DSASweep · rooms = heap size

A min-heap of end times.

Meeting rooms II: minimum rooms so no two overlapping meetings share one. Sort by start. A min-heap holds end times of rooms currently busy. When a meeting starts, pop every room that already finished (end ≤ start). Push this end. The heap’s peak size is the answer. Chronological sweep: +1 at start, −1 at end, scan a max running count — same.

Reuse the room that frees first

Heap of ends

[0,30] [5,10] [15,20] push 30 size 1 5 < 30, push 10 size 2 10 ≤ 15, pop 10 push 20 size 2 max size = 2

Pop on ≤, not <: a meeting that ends at 10 frees the room for a 10 start.

or

+1 start · −1 end

Sweep

(0,+1) (5,+1) (10,-1) (15,+1) (20,-1) (30,-1) running 1, 2, 1, 2, 1, 0 max 2 tie at t: process ends first (reuse)

Rooms I is easier: one person, sort-and-check no overlap. Rooms II is the count of concurrent.

What

Greedy: reuse the room that frees first. Heap size = rooms in use. O(n log n). The heap stores ends, not starts — you need “when is the next room free?”

Why

LeetCode 253 / lintcode. SDE-2 interval. Plate 115 is the heap; this is the meeting. “Can one person attend all?” is rooms I — no heap.

Trap

Sorting by duration. Heap of starts. Using < vs ≤ at equal timestamps. n² “assign to first free room” without a heap. Merging intervals (plate 157) — that unions them, it does not count rooms.
