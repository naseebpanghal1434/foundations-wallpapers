NetworkConnection: keep-alive

Keep-alive is reuse, not magic.

Keep-alive (HTTP/1.1 default) leaves the TCP/TLS socket open for the next request, skipping handshake and slow start (plates 78, 276). Idle timeouts on the load balancer, NAT, and origin will still kill it.

GET, FIN, GET

Two TLS

GET /a 200 FIN TCP + TLS again GET /b 200 Connection: close ends the socket

You paid the handshake twice. A pool of idle sockets is the real design (plate 3).

vs

GET, GET · same fd

Reuse

GET /a 200 GET /b 200 same fd H1: one at a time H2: reuse + multiplex

Timeouts must agree. Proxies may coalesce or not (plate 286).

What

Reuse the session. Client, load balancer, and server idle timers must agree. Recycle idle connections on purpose.

Why

APIs, browsers, gRPC channels. Tail latency is often a new handshake, not the handler.

Trap

Infinite idle. Mismatched timeouts: NAT 60s, you think 5 minutes — mystery resets. One giant H1 keep-alive and a greedy neighbor blocking the line.
