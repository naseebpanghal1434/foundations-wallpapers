DSASame tree, three sequences

Pre, in, post. Inorder is sorted.

Same BST: 4, left 2 (1 and 3), right 5. Preorder visits the node first, then left, then right. Inorder: left, node, right. Postorder: both children, then the node. On a BST, inorder is the keys in sorted order. That is the fact they want.

Node, left, right

Preorder

4 2 1 3 5
Root first. Copy, serialize, prefix of a path.
Not sorted. The 4 is not “in the middle.”

Left, node, right

Inorder

1 2 3 4 5
BST → ascending keys. Validate, kth, range.
Arbitrary tree: just “left then node.” Not sorted.

Left, right, node

Postorder

1 3 2 5 4
Children before parent. Delete, eval an expression.
Root is last. 4 is not the start.
What

Three recursive walks, one line each. Iterative: stack for pre/in, two stacks or a reverse-pre for post. Rebuild: preorder + inorder (or post + inorder) uniquely determine a binary tree if keys are unique.

Why

Interviews start here because every later tree problem is “when do I look at the node?” BST inorder = sorted is the one-sentence check that you know what a BST is (plate 58).

Trap

Mixing pre and in: writing node, left, right and calling it inorder; saying this tree’s inorder is 4 2 1 3 5 (that’s pre). Or claiming inorder is sorted on any binary tree — only on a BST.
