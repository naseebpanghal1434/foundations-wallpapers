DatabaseBEGIN · WAL fsync

ACID is four promises.

Four separate guarantees a transaction asks of the engine — not a vibe. BEGIN … COMMIT / ROLLBACK. Interviews fail people who recite the acronym and cannot assign a sentence, a syscall, or a constraint to each letter.

A · atomic

all / none

Debit A and credit B live or die together. ROLLBACK exists. Plate 07.

C · consistent

PK FK CHECK

Constraints hold at COMMIT: PRIMARY KEY, FOREIGN KEY, UNIQUE, CHECK. Not CAP-C (plate 11).

I · isolated

RC…SSI

What others see. Levels (plate 40). More isolation, more waits or 40001 retries.

D · durable

WAL + fsync

After COMMIT returns, a crash must not eat it. synchronous_commit=on. Power-cord test.

What

A = rollback. C = declared constraints, not “the data makes business sense” (that’s the app). I = levels. D = WAL fsynced to disk. CAP-C is “all nodes see the same write.” ACID-C is “the schema’s rules still hold.”

Why

How you talk about Postgres vs Redis vs a queue. Redis can be durable-ish (AOF, fsync everysec); it is not your FK enforcer. Know which promise you just gave up.

Trap

Using C for business sense. Claiming durable with fsync=off or synchronous_commit=off — that’s speed, not D. Unlogged tables, same story.
