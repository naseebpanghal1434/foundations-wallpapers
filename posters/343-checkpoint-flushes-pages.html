Databasecheckpoint_timeout · spread

Checkpoint flushes dirty pages. WAL already is durable.

WAL (plate 95) is the durability path: COMMIT = fsync the log. Dirty 8 KiB heap pages sit in shared_buffers until a checkpoint writes them; then older WAL can be recycled. checkpoint_timeout 5 min, max_wal_size. A spike of heap writes at checkpoint hurts.

Fsync the heap on COMMIT

Death

COMMIT fsync page 4 fsync page 9 fsync page 18 … random 8 KiB writes on the hot path

That is not how Postgres COMMITs. The log is sequential; the heap waits.

vs

Two clocks

Checkpoint

COMMIT fsync WAL dirty pages later checkpoint_timeout 5min completion_target 0.9 spread the heap I/O crash → redo WAL from last checkpoint full_page_writes after it

Redo LSN vs WAL insert. Confusing checkpoint with COMMIT is the interview miss.

What

Checkpoint = heap caught up to here; older WAL recyclable. First change to a page after a checkpoint logs a full 8 KiB image (full_page_writes) against torn pages.

Why

Latency spikes every five minutes. Tune max_wal_size vs timeout so checkpoints spread, not slam.

Trap

checkpoint_completion_target 0 and a 30s I/O storm. Disabling full_page_writes on a filesystem without torn-page protection.
