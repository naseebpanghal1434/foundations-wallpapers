System4 KiB pages · 48-bit VA

The address is not the RAM.

A pointer is a virtual address, not a DRAM pin. The MMU walks a page table — x86-64: 4 KiB pages, 4-level, 48-bit canonical VA — to a physical frame. A miss is #PF: the kernel fills from file, swap, or a zero page. Two processes can both have 0x400000. Huge pages are 2 MiB / 1 GiB.

Page

4 KiB

VA bits: VPN | offset. PTE: present? writable? PFN. 2 MiB huge pages skip a level.

Walk

4-level

CR3 → PML4 → PDPT → PD → PT. TLB caches the translation. Process switch may flush it (plate 80).

Resident

RSS vs VSZ

VSZ = the virtual map (can be huge). RSS = pages in RAM. mmap 4 GiB without using 4 GiB until you touch it.

Miss

#PF

Minor: already in RAM, just map. Major: disk / swap. Overcommit: malloc succeeds, later fault is OOM SIGKILL.

What

Isolation (plate 75) is this map. Swap is overflow; thrash is working set > RAM and the CPU idle in iowait. OOM is the kernel picking a victim.

Why

Interviews: page, fault, swap, thrash, RSS vs virtual. “Why did it die after malloc succeeded?” lives here. Linux overcommits by default.

Trap

Only thinking “malloc returned NULL.” You get a pointer; the later page fault is SIGKILL from the OOM killer. Also: ignoring thrash because CPU looks idle — it is waiting on disk.
