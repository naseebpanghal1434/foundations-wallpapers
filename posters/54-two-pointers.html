DSATwo ends · or a chase

Opposite ends, or slow and fast.

Two indices walk a sequence instead of nested loops. Opposite ends: lo++ / hi-- on a sorted array. Slow/fast: one step vs two on a list. Each index moves at most n times → O(n). The array for two-sum-from-ends has to already be sorted.

Sorted pair / palindrome

Opposite ends

lo, hi = 0, n-1 while lo < hi: s = a[lo] + a[hi] if s == t: hit if s < t: lo++ else: hi--

Invariant: if a pair exists, it sits in [lo, hi]. Also: container-with-most-water, in-place reverse.

or

Cycle / middle of a list

Slow / fast

slow = fast = head while fast and fast.next: slow = slow.next fast = fast.next.next # meet → cycle (Floyd) # else slow is the mid

Fast hits null → no cycle. Same-direction variant: left writes, right reads (dups, partition).

What

Replace O(n²) pair checks with a monotonic walk. Ends-in needs order (or a palindrome). Slow/fast needs a next pointer. Sliding window (plate 55) is two pointers that only move forward.

Why

SDE-1 greatest hits: 2-sum, 3-sum after sort, linked-list cycle, middle node, remove dups in-place. Say which pointer moves, and why the other may stay.

Trap

Unsorted 2-sum with two ends. Without order, shrinking the range is a guess — you skip the pair. Unsorted 2-sum is a hash map (indices stay). Sorting first is O(n log n) and loses indices unless you store them. Moving both pointers when you should move one.
