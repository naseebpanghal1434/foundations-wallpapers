NetworkUDP/53 · TTL seconds

A name becomes an IP.

DNS maps a hostname to an address: api.shop.io A 203.0.113.10 (AAAA for IPv6). The stub asks a recursive resolver (UDP/53, or DoT 853 / DoH 443); the recursor walks root → TLD → auth NS and caches each RR for its TTL. getaddrinfo(3) is the libc call. TTL 86400 = a day of stale answers after you move the IP.

Address

A / AAAA

api.shop.io A 203.0.113.10
AAAA 2001:db8::10. What TCP dials. Plate 13 starts here.

Alias

CNAME

www CNAME shop.cdn.net then another lookup. Cannot sit next to an A at the zone apex.

Cache

TTL 86400

Resolver, OS, browser each keep a copy. Drop to 300 before cutover. NXDOMAIN ≠ SERVFAIL.

What

Stub → recursor → root → TLD → auth NS. Also MX, NS, TXT. NXDOMAIN is “no such name.” SERVFAIL is “the lookup broke.” Negative answers have their own TTL (SOA minimum).

Why

A site that “is down” is often a name still pointing at a dead box, or a cache that hasn’t expired. Deploy is not done until DNS and its TTL agree.

Trap

Cutover with TTL 86400 — resolvers keep the old A for a day. Flushing only your laptop and calling DNS “updated.” Also: CNAME at the apex next to SOA/NS, which most zones forbid.
