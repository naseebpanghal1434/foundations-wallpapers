NetworkHead-of-line · TCP vs H2

One stall. The whole line waits.

Head-of-line blocking: a lost packet at the front freezes everything behind it. HTTP/2 multiplexes streams, but TCP’s single ordered byte stream still HOL-blocks all of them on one loss; HTTP/3/QUIC makes loss per-stream (plate 275).

Pipelining on H1 is not multiplex — later responses still wait on the first.

HTTP/2 over TCP

TCP HOL

streams A and B A frame lost TCP holds the seq B’s frames sit behind A all streams freeze

This is why H3 exists, not because UDP is fashionable. Multiplex does not cancel TCP order.

What

HOL at HTTP and HOL at TCP are different layers. H2 fixed the HTTP line, not the TCP line.

Why

Lossy wifi. One fat upload ruining the tab. Don’t pin unrelated calls on one fragile flow — or move to H3.

Trap

“H2 fixed HOL.” One TCP to a sick peer and every stream freezes. Treating H1 pipelining as if it were multiplex.
