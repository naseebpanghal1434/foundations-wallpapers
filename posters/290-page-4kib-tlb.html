OSPTE · TLB · huge pages

A page is 4 KiB. The TLB caches translations.

Virtual address = VPN + offset. Offset is 12 bits → 4096-byte pages (x86). The page table (4 levels on 48-bit x86-64) maps VPN → PFN + flags (R/W, U/S, NX, dirty). The TLB caches PTEs; a miss walks memory (plate 79).

Page

4 KiB

12-bit offset. Default granularity.

Huge pages

2 MiB

Also 1 GiB. 512× fewer PTEs per 2 MiB.

Canonical VA

48-bit

4-level walk. 5-level is 57-bit opt-in.

Walk

~4 refs

One cache line per level on a miss.

L1 DTLB

~64

Entries. Miss → walk. munmap shoots down (INVLPG / IPI).

THP/hugetlbfs opt into 2 MiB/1 GiB. Internal fragmentation ≤ 4 KiB−1 per mapping. Sparse VAs are cheap until you touch them (plate 291).

What

Paging granularity is 4 KiB unless you opt into huge pages. The TLB is the cache of that map.

Why

Why malloc of 1 byte still costs a page the first time (plus metadata). Why a 64 GiB scan with 4 KiB pages is 16M PTEs and a TLB thrash.

Trap

Assuming stack/heap grow by bytes. Walking tens of GiB on 4 KiB pages — use huge pages, or don’t. mmap/munmap without expecting TLB shootdowns.
