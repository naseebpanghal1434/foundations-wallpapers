FrontendStrict · Lax · None

SameSite is the CSRF cookie.

SameSite tells the browser when to attach a cookie on cross-site requests (CSRF, plate 30). Lax (default) sends it on top-level GET, not on cross-site POST; Strict never; None always — and must be Secure.

Never cross-site

Strict

no cookie on any cross-site including a link to you

Clicking from email into your app arrives logged out. First-party only.

Default · CSRF seatbelt

Lax

link GET: cookie evil POST: no subresource GET: no

Top-level GET still sends it. Cross-site fetch/POST does not. Site = eTLD+1, not origin.

Always · must be Secure

None

None; Secure iframes / APIs token still required

Cross-site SPA → api.other.com needs None, or no cookies (Authorization header).

What

Same-site ≠ same-origin. Lax is the CSRF seatbelt for cookie sessions. app.foo.com vs bar.foo.com is same-site.

Why

Modern CSRF. A token is still wise for None and for extra-sensitive POST.

Trap

SameSite=None without Secure — rejected. Thinking Lax blocks a GET /delete?id= from an <img> — Lax sends cookies on top-level GET, not on subresource GET.
