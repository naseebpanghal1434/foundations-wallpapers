ArchIOPS · sequential

HDD seeks ~10 ms. NVMe is ~100 µs.

HDD: mechanical seek+rotation 5–15 ms → ~100 IOPS. SATA SSD: ~100 µs, 10k–100k IOPS. NVMe: 20–100 µs, 100k–1M IOPS. Sequential GB/s on all of them if you stop seeking. RAM ~100 ns latency, 10–50 GB/s, ~10M random ops/s/core if you miss cache.

10–50 GB/s. Page cache is this, not disk.

fsync on HDD is a seek; on NVMe still 10–100 µs. RAID and queues (iodepth) exist because one thread cannot fill NVMe. Page is 4 KiB.

What

IOPS vs bandwidth are different axes. Random 4K vs sequential 1M differ by 100× on HDD, ~5–10× on SSD.

Why

Why databases care about fsync (plate 95). Why log-structured merge trees exist.

Trap

Benchmarking with /dev/null or the page cache (that is RAM ~100 ns). One fsync per row insert.
