NetworkStreams · one TCP

HTTP/2 multiplexes. HTTP/1 lined up.

HTTP/1.1 is one request at a time per connection — pipelining never really worked — so browsers open ~6 TCPs per host. HTTP/2 is many streams on one TLS connection; head-of-line still exists at TCP (plate 285), which is why HTTP/3 moved to QUIC.

Six connections · GETs in line

HTTP/1.1

TCP1 GET /app.css wait TCP2 GET /app.js wait TCP3 GET /icon.svg wait … ×6 per host one request per socket until the response ends

Keep-alive (plate 277) reuses the socket. It does not interleave requests.

vs

One TLS · streams 1, 3, 5

HTTP/2

1 TLS ALPN h2 sid 1 /app.js sid 3 /app.css sid 5 /icon.svg in flight together binary frames · HPACK

A fat image still occupies bytes; multiplex is not extra bandwidth. Server push mostly lost — preload instead.

What

One TLS, many streams, binary frames. HPACK compresses headers. Priorities exist; implementations vary.

Why

Domain-sharding is harmful on H2 — you split what should share one connection. Plate 13 is the click; this is the reuse.

Trap

Sharding assets across 8 hosts on HTTP/2, or assuming multiplexed means no blocking. HTTP/1.1 keepalive (plate 277) is not multiplexing.
